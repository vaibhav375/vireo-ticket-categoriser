**Re:** Headcount chart — where the two hires should go

**The short answer:** Billing is not your biggest queue. It looks biggest because the chat bot
files "I paid but my order hasn't arrived" as a payment problem. Fix that first. If you still
need hires after that, they belong in Logistics.

**What we found**

We read all 11,641 tickets from January 2025 to June 2026 and sorted each by what the customer
actually needed, not by the bot's tag. We checked each result against the agent's own closing note.

| Share of tickets | By the bot's tag | By what the customer needed |
|---|---|---|
| Billing | 21% | **14%** |
| Logistics | 16% | **26%** |

- **39% of the Billing queue is delivery work.** The customer mentions paying, so the bot sends the
  ticket to Billing. Billing then either passes it to Logistics or chases the courier itself.
- **Those customers get a worse experience.** Compared with the same kind of ticket sent straight
  to Logistics, they breach the first-response promise 4 times as often (36% vs 9%), wait longer
  for a fix (49 hours vs 32), and rate us lower (2.3 vs 3.1 out of 5).
- **Neha is right about Logistics.** Your reports charge a late first reply to whoever *closes*
  the ticket. So Logistics is blamed for delays that happened while tickets sat in the Billing queue.
- **Per person, Logistics carries the most.** Counting only work that is really theirs, each
  Logistics agent handles about 37 tickets a month in this export, against 27 for Billing and
  24 for Returns. (Warranty is specialist work and isn't compared on volume.)

Across all teams, **17% of tickets go to the wrong team first**. At your volume of about 650 tickets
a week, the extra transfers and late-reply credits alone cost about **Rs 2.1 lakh a quarter**.
That doesn't count the lower ratings.

**What we recommend**

1. **Fix the front door this month.** Either add one question to the bot ("Has your order
   arrived?") or have our tool read the customer's first message and route it. The tool is built
   and tested. Sameer would need to connect it.
2. **Hold the two hires for eight weeks** and re-run this chart monthly. The goal:
   **cut wrong-team tickets from 17% to 5%, worth about Rs 1.5 lakh a quarter**. For scale, the
   two hires cost about Rs 2.25 lakh a quarter. This is Arjun's "fix the process before hiring into it".
3. **If you still need people after that, start with Logistics.** Billing will have lost more
   than a third of its queue, so it will likely have spare capacity.

**How sure are we**

We trained the tool on tickets up to March 2026 and tested it on April–June, which it had never
seen. It agreed with the agents' own notes on 2,162 of 2,163 tickets. The bot's tag agreed on 71%.
The tougher test is how customers word a problem in ways the tool has never seen: it was right about
9 times in 10. It can also tell when it's unsure. Sending the uncertain ones (about one in eight) to a person makes it 94% right on the rest.
We also judged a random 150 tickets from both the message and the agent's note. The tool matched on all 150; the bot's tag on 101.

Two cautions. First, your exported messages are very consistent, and live messages will be messier,
so we'd re-check 50 tickets a month. Second, that 150-ticket check was done on our side
with AI assistance, so a Vireo team lead should spot-check it before you decide.

**One thing for Arjun, found on the way:** 139 orders received both a refund and a replacement,
which your policy says should never happen. 73 look like genuine double payouts (the refund
code says the customer was refunded for a return, dead-on-arrival or lost parcel, and a replacement
also went out), about Rs 1.4 lakh in replacement cost. Where a ticket didn't quote an order number we
matched it by customer and product, so a few links may be wrong. We haven't verified these; Finance should look at the list.

**What this doesn't cover:** shift-by-shift staffing and product-defect analysis. A quick look found
no clear signal in either, and neither changes the headcount answer.
